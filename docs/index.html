03 / PURPOSE OF THE EXPERIMENT

Why this experiment exists

One day. Several physical processes. One number to remember.

THE GOAL OF THE EXPERIMENT

Detect a change in the timeline and obtain proof that someone has created a time machine.

TimeLineStamp turns a day's physical measurements into a number. If it changes for the same UTC day while an observer remembers the previous value, we will regard the timeline as having changed. These data cannot tell us exactly when that happened.

POINT OF COMPARISON
NUMBER IN MEMORYthe observer's recollection
NUMBER NOWthe record for the same UTC day
Do the two readings match?
WHY A RANDOMIZER

A random outcome as a mark of history

Quantum beacons publish the results of physical measurements. We sample their pulses every five minutes. If a day's events unfold differently, the sequence of outcomes may differ. One pulse marks only a moment; the daily fingerprint combines hundreds of pulses with solar wind data.

WHY THESE DATA

We combine events on very different scales: individual quantum measurements and the changing conditions between the Sun and Earth.

01 / NIST + NQSN

Quantum events

Quantum generators derive random bits from physical measurements. The two beacons are run by different operators, so the daily record does not depend on a single instrument or location. We take one pulse from each every five minutes.

02 / NOAA MAG

Magnetic field

The solar wind carries a magnetic field. Its strength and direction change with solar activity and the motion of plasma. Minute-by-minute readings add the history of a large, restless system to the fingerprint.

03 / NOAA PLASMA

Particle flow

Proton speed, density, and temperature describe another side of the same solar wind. Together with its magnetic field, they give a fuller record of what happened on the way to Earth that day.

Reading Steiner names a situation in which the observer remembers the earlier reading after a possible shift in the world line, while physical records already belong to another history. That is why this experiment compares memory with the page as it appears now.

After the daily seal, remember the coefficient and its UTC date. Later, open the record for that date again. If the number you remember differs from the one shown, report the discrepancy. That is the sign this experiment is looking for.

06 / METHOD

How the fingerprint is made

A day of measurements becomes a verifiable digital record and a short coefficient.

TimeLineStamp collects public physical measurements over one UTC day. The server stores the original responses and seals the record once a day. The published coefficient is a short marker for that record; its full verification fingerprint is a SHA-256 hash.

01

Period and sampling

NIST and NQSN each provide an expected 288 pulses: one every five minutes. NOAA provides minute-by-minute magnetic field and plasma series: up to 1,440 records per stream each day.

02

Sealing

At 00:15 UTC the next day, a canonical record is built. Empty beacon slots remain null. A sealed day is not recalculated; substantial gaps are marked separately.

03

Verification

SHA-256 is calculated from the full record. The coefficient is deterministically derived from the hash, so verification requires both the UTC date and the full hash. Version 1 does not yet verify beacon signatures.

SOURCE 03 / NOAA SWPC1 MIN / UP TO 1,440

Magnetic field

The stream rtsw_mag_1m describes the interplanetary magnetic field in the solar wind. The record includes the field magnitude Bt and the components Bx, By, Bz in GSM coordinates when the row is active and its overall quality flag is zero.

SOURCE 04 / NOAA SWPC1 MIN / UP TO 1,440

Solar wind plasma

The stream rtsw_wind_1m describes solar wind protons: their speed, density, and temperature. When DSCOVR is active, these values come from its Faraday Cup plasma instrument. As with the magnetic stream, TimeLineStamp takes only active rows with a good overall quality flag.
